I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the London School of Economics, currently a visiting PhD student at UC San Diego. My research is in labor and health economics, with interests in development and macroeconomics.

Before the LSE, I was a predoctoral fellow at Yale University, where I worked with Michael Peters and Fabrizio Zilibotti. I hold an MSc in Economics from University College London and a double degree in Economics and International Studies from Carlos III University of Madrid.

Research

Can Labor Market Opportunities Explain the Rising Levels of Morbidity and Mortality in the U.S.? Working paper, 2025 · SSRN

Using the opening of large plants across U.S. counties (1982–1993) as a natural experiment, I estimate the causal effect of improved local labor-market conditions on “deaths of despair,” finding sizeable and persistent reductions in overdose mortality, concentrated among men and midlife adults.
